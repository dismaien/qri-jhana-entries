# Draft Submission: Somatic Arc

Working draft for the written description. The practice-context paragraph now uses a nondual Shaiva Tantra framing rather than claiming a specific attainment history.

## Draft

This piece models the first four jhanas as changes in the internal dynamics of a single somatic field rather than as four unrelated images. The body remains visible throughout because I wanted the transition sequence itself to communicate something: the felt sense of embodiment is not removed all at once. Instead, the body gradually changes from a corrugated, eventful, attention-grabbing object into a smoother, more uniform volume of sensation.

In the first segment, corresponding to jhana 1, the strongest activity is concentrated in the chest, throat, and head. I used a warm gold and ember palette, short serrated flow lines, prickle-like tactile marks, and unstable local turbulence to represent pīti as a rapturous, somewhat electric activation that is beginning to lock attention in place. The darker troughs between the bright strands are meant to imply that attention has not yet saturated the whole body evenly. It is a field with active ignition points rather than an already-balanced absorption.

The second segment, jhana 2, keeps the brightness but changes the logic of the motion. The energy is less effortful and more self-propelling. I lowered turbulence, increased phase coherence, and redistributed weight across the whole avatar so the image feels less like trying and more like being carried. The body is still radiant and vivid, but the visual story is no longer one of forcing contact. It is closer to a resonant loop that sustains itself.

In jhana 3 the field cools. The image shifts toward cyan and pale turquoise, the pulse rate drops, and the directional lines become broader and less urgent. The tactile marks become more pressure-like and laminar, as if individual sparkles were resolving into a smoother field of bodily ease. I wanted this section to feel placid rather than triumphant: pleasure is still present, but it is softer, quieter, and less sticky. The sensation field spreads more evenly through the abdomen and lower body, reducing the top-heavy feel of the earlier segments.

In jhana 4 I compress contrast and event density so the visual field becomes almost membrane-like. There is still luminosity, but less drama. The body is rendered as a near-uniform equilibrium with minimal bias or push. Lines remain only as a faint trace of balanced order, and the segment envelope gradually settles turbulence and line length so the motion itself feels less acquisitive. The goal was to suggest equanimity not as deadness, but as a highly coherent low-preference state.

Technically, the piece is generated from a set of coupled oscillator nodes placed through the avatar and aura. Each segment adjusts coherence, turbulence, node weights, pulse rate, and an attack/sustain/release-style envelope. A clipped contour layer gives the avatar more rounded internal volume, while a tactile overlay changes mark type across the sequence. The intention is to translate first-person meditative texture into a field language of synchronization and flow without reducing the experience to a mere diagram.

Practice context: my background for this visual model is not a Theravada-only jhana protocol, but a nondual Shaiva Tantra practice context shaped by Tantra Illuminated and related Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra material. The practices I had in mind include breath and pause-based centering, heart and body-based awareness, mantra or subtle sound as a way of gathering attention, and the Tantrik move of recognizing sensations as expressions of a single field of awareness rather than obstacles to be excluded. For that reason, this piece should be read as a cross-tradition phenomenological translation: the jhana labels provide the contest structure, while the visual route emphasizes embodiment, energy, and recognition. The avatar is not intended as an anatomical body or a chakra diagram. It is a way to show how sensation can become increasingly coherent when attention, affect, and somatic intensity are allowed to organize as one field.
