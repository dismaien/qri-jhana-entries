## Draft

This piece treats the formless jhanas as a progressive loss of normal object-like structure. I wanted the transitions to be legible without relying on a body avatar, so the composition is built from spatial curvature, recursion, subtraction, and event density rather than anatomy.

The jhana 5 segment emphasizes openness. The image is structured by elliptical spatial bands and edge-launched field lines that are meant to read more like boundless depth than like a scene full of things. The visual aim is figure-ground inversion: the most salient feature is not an object in space, but the quality of space itself. The field remains coherent enough to feel stable, but it begins to organize the viewer around barrierlessness rather than containment.

Jhana 6 introduces reflexivity. The circular and elliptical structures fold back toward the center, and the flow lines become more self-referential. I added a lensing pass so faint paths bend, shear, and re-enter the field instead of merely orbiting a static center. I wanted this section to suggest awareness becoming aware of itself, not by adding a face or eye symbol, but by increasing the degree to which the geometry appears to mirror and re-enter itself. The scene should feel less like looking outward and more like the medium of knowing recognizing its own activity.

In jhana 7, stable landing zones are intentionally removed. The center is darkened, the attractors are weakened, and the event density drops. The segment envelope also thins the line work over time, so the field does not merely depict absence as a static dark shape. The image is no longer spacious in the same affirmative way as jhana 5. Instead it is defined by the absence of something to rest on. The field still has coherence, but it resists presenting an object or center worthy of grasping.

Jhana 8 is treated as a near-failure of depiction. I reduced line count, pulse rate, and contrast until only a faint threshold remains. The remaining traces are deliberately thin and ambiguous. This segment is supposed to feel like the barest possible persistence of perceptual structure rather than a full scene.

All four segments are generated from the same coupled-oscillator engine, but the topology shifts from body-centered coordinates to field-centered coordinates. The shared envelope system gradually opens, recurses, subtracts, and attenuates the field. This continuity matters to me because it frames the formless jhanas not as a different universe, but as a further refinement and attenuation of the same dynamical substrate.

Practice context: this piece is informed by nondual Shaiva Tantra as taught through Tantra Illuminated and by the practice orientation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, especially the way attention can open through space, sensation, thought, sound, darkness, delight, and the ordinary field of experience. In that context, formlessness is not treated as a rejection of manifestation, but as a shift in how manifestation is known: the apparent object becomes less important than the aware space, reflexivity, and luminous ground in which it appears. The piece therefore avoids body imagery and instead uses curvature, recursion, disappearance, and sparse traces to model a movement from spaciousness toward near-threshold perception. The jhana labels are used as the contest map, while the visual grammar leans on a Tantrik recognition frame: awareness recognizes its own activity, then progressively releases the need to stabilize around an object.
